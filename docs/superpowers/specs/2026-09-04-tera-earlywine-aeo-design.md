# Tera Earlywine AEO and Lead-Generation Design

## Goal

Turn `teraearlywine.com` into the canonical public source for Tera Earlywine as an independent consultant who builds production-grade data and AI systems for regulated, high-consequence operations, while converting qualified enterprise buyers into 20–30 minute fit calls.

## Audience

The primary audience is a data, engineering, compliance, risk, or operations leader at a U.S. fintech, B2B SaaS company, marketplace, or adjacent enterprise. The best-fit company has roughly 100–2,000 employees and a costly reliability, migration, governance, or AI-productionization problem.

## Positioning

The lead category is deliberately precise: **production-grade data and AI for regulated, high-consequence operations**. Precision is an acquisition wedge, not an exclusion rule. Supporting copy must also make Tera discoverable for data-platform modernization, migration rescue, AI-ready data foundations, agentic-AI productionization, governance, reliability, and fractional platform leadership.

Tera Earlywine is the primary public entity. Idea Factory may appear as the business vehicle, but it must not compete with Tera's name or reposition her as a small-business web studio.

## Voice

Copy must sound casual, direct, technically credible, and human. It should avoid consultant fog, inflated claims, generic transformation language, and keyword-stuffed prose. Every claim must either be factual public biography, a sanitized outcome, or a clearly described service.

## Homepage Flow

1. **Hero:** State the buyer problem, specialty, and credibility in plain English. Provide a primary `Book a fit call` action and a secondary path to proof.
2. **Commercial proof:** Surface the strongest sanitized outcomes: 9+ years in data, platform work spanning 500+ workflows, $500K+ in infrastructure savings, and leadership across Block, Mercari, and consulting engagements.
3. **Problems Tera solves:** Reliability and migration rescue; AI-ready data foundations; production AI systems; fractional platform leadership.
4. **Selected work:** Describe named, sanitized work without exposing confidential systems, incidents, or client data.
5. **Delivery model:** Explain Diagnose → Blueprint → Prove → Scale → Operate so buyers understand how an engagement begins and grows.
6. **About and experience:** Preserve the real career chronology while making independent consulting the current primary identity.
7. **FAQ:** Answer the buyer and answer-engine questions directly: who Tera is, what she does, who she works with, when to hire her, and how to start.
8. **Conversion:** Repeat one primary CTA: `Book a fit call`. Retain the secure contact form as a lower-priority alternative.

## Answer-Engine Signals

- Use one consistent name, title, description, and specialty across visible copy and metadata.
- Extend `Person` and `ProfessionalService` structured data with expertise, services, employer history, and same-as profiles that the page visibly supports.
- Add `FAQPage` structured data whose answers exactly match visible FAQ content.
- Preserve canonical URLs, sitemap, robots directives, Search Console verification, and privacy-safe analytics.
- Align the public GitHub profile README with the consulting identity and canonical site.
- Avoid claims of guaranteed ranking, citation, or lead volume.

## Conversion and Measurement

The primary conversion is a booking-link click for a 20–30 minute fit call. The contact form remains available for buyers who prefer email. Existing privacy-safe events remain intact; new hero and repeated booking CTAs use the current `contact_click` contract.

Success will be measured over 90 days using qualified fit calls, consulting inquiries, branded and category-query visibility, citations/mentions, booking CTA clicks, and contact submissions. Rankings and answer-engine citations are lagging observations, not launch blockers.

## Constraints

- Preserve the existing Flask architecture, contact-delivery safety, analytics consent behavior, accessibility, and mobile responsiveness.
- Do not publish confidential client details, internal system names, incident data, or unsupported attribution.
- Do not add a new lead magnet, newsletter, database, or authentication flow.
- Do not replace the current hosting stack in this change.

## Rollout

Phase one is the canonical authority hub: homepage, metadata, structured data, GitHub alignment, and test coverage. Phase two is repeatable corroboration: case studies, LinkedIn articles, guest appearances, and third-party profile cleanup. Phase three measures discovery-to-call conversion and tightens the category based on actual qualified demand.
